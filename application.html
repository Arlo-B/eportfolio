Application

Cover letter for the Industrus Engineering Graduate Program, addressing each of the six selection criteria using the STAR method.

Dear Graduate Recruitment Team,

I am writing to apply for a position in the Industrus Engineering Graduate Program. I am completing a Bachelor of Engineering (Honours) in Mechanical and Mechatronic Engineering at UTS, and I bring three years of full-cycle engineering delivery with the UTS Motorsports Formula SAE team, where I progressed from team member to Chassis Lead and then to Mechanical Lead, alongside current professional work as a data and race engineer in GT3 competition. What I offer Industrus is an engineer who closes the loop between design and hardware: I take systems from concept through CAD, analysis and design for manufacture, then build, test and remain accountable for whether they work, and I can lead a team while doing it. A program that rotates graduates across project roles and divisions is exactly the environment I want, because the breadth I gained running a whole vehicle is the breadth I want to keep building on.

My responses to the selection criteria follow.

A commitment to ethical conduct and the highest standards of professional accountability (3.1)

As Mechanical Lead for the 2025 Formula SAE electric vehicle, I owned rules compliance and safety-critical sign-off, including the 600 V accumulator enclosure. The two previous UTS cars had failed technical scrutineering, which is the safety gate a car must clear before it is allowed to compete. Rather than treat compliance as a final check, I built the FSAE technical regulations into the design reviews from the start, reviewed every subsystem against the rule set, verified calculations with destructive material testing, and kept the documentation needed to defend each decision. Accountability also meant owning my own errors: when I found the tubes coming out of the bender did not match my CAD, because I had designed to the tool's nominal bend radius instead of its measured one, I said so, measured the machine, compensated in the model and made pre-design measurement of tooling a standing step. Where a part did not meet specification, it was remade, even when the schedule made that expensive. The result was the first UTS Motorsports car in years to pass all technical scrutineering, and a car I was confident enough in to drive myself in competition.

Demonstrated ability to effectively communicate both with other engineers and with stakeholders from different fields (3.2)

Leading roughly twenty engineers across chassis, suspension, drivetrain, tractive, braking, cooling and packaging meant most of my work was translation. I chaired weekly team meetings and ran design reviews where mechanical constraints had to be made intelligible to electrical and software members, and their requirements back to mine. Outside the team I reported to faculty and to sponsors including Schaeffler, Ansys and Altium, who are stakeholders with commercial rather than technical interests. I have also presented the team's work publicly twice at SXSW Sydney: as an invited speaker on the autonomous vehicle programme in 2023, and across two days at the 2025 technology showcase, including a livestreamed radio interview about the car and the project. The outcome was a build in which subsystem interfaces held together through integration, and a sponsor network that stayed with the team across the campaign.

The ability to engage with a creative, innovative and proactive environment (3.3)

When I took over the mechanical effort, the team had two consecutive campaigns that had not reached the track, and the problem was not any single component. I proposed changing how we worked rather than what we built: interfaces between subsystems tracked explicitly, design reviews held on a schedule, and compliance designed in rather than checked at the end. That change in approach produced the team's first endurance finish since 2019. I also build outside anything I am asked to. My Year 12 project, a motorcycle e-safety vest using haptic pods to give riders an extra sense of surrounding traffic, was recognised as top in the state and displayed at the Powerhouse Museum. Since then I have refurbished a postie bike and given it phone-operated start and a movement alarm running on an ESP32, rebuilt an engine specifically to test whether Nomex works as a general-purpose gasket material, and I am developing an omni-wheel intended to be more durable than conventional designs. At Tigani Motorsport the same instinct produced a race video and data splitter that turns a session into per-driver stints and pit stop timings automatically, which did not exist before I wrote it.

Demonstrated ability to use and manage information (3.4)

I work as a data and race engineer with Tigani Motorsport in GT3 competition, where my role is to turn race telemetry into setup and strategy decisions under time pressure. I configure the Bosch MS6.2 ECUs in RaceCon, build the MoTeC i2 Pro and WinDarab dashboards and maths channels the engineers work from, and built the in-car Raspberry Pi gateway that carries CAN and serial telemetry and video over LTE into a live stream the team can view remotely. I also designed the team's server, its WireGuard VPN and its access control model, so that data is available to the people who need it and only to them. At UTS Motorsports I applied the same discipline to design: Ansys FEA to predict behaviour, then destructive and non-destructive testing to check whether the prediction held, with results recorded so later revisions started from evidence rather than assumption. The practical result is that I treat data as something to be managed, secured and handed on, not just consumed.

The ability to manage your own performance in a professional environment (3.5)

For three years I treated UTS Motorsports as a professional commitment, averaging around eight and a half hours a day, five days a week, and fitting a full-time engineering degree around it. Sustaining that required managing my own workload deliberately: setting the build schedule, deciding what had to happen before a deadline and what could wait, and protecting the time needed for my studies at the same time. I did this across three consecutive campaigns without dropping either commitment, delivering the 2024 and 2025 builds while remaining on track to graduate in 2026, and have since added professional trackside work to the same schedule.

A demonstrated ability to work as part of a team and to show leadership when required (3.6)

I began as a team member in 2023 and moved into leadership because the team needed it. As Chassis Lead and then Mechanical Lead I interviewed a large pool of applicants at each intake, onboarded and trained new recruits, and built a retained core of around twenty effective contributors, including supervising students completing their own internship requirements. The part I found hardest, and most valuable, was learning to break a problem down for someone, let them attempt it, and review the result without simply taking it back. That is what made the 2025 campaign possible: a car that passed scrutineering, completed every dynamic event, and placed 8th in Endurance, 8th in Autocross and 10th overall in the EV class. I have stayed on in 2026 as a mechanical and team advisor so the knowledge does not leave with me.

I would welcome the opportunity to discuss my application with you. Thank you for your consideration.

Yours sincerely,
Arlo Berry
