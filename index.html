About me

I build things. I came to engineering because I wanted to understand how physical objects actually get made, not just modelled, calculated and signed off, but cut, welded, assembled and then tested hard enough to find out whether the design was any good. That started with a shed I built with my father when I was fifteen, and with a Year 12 project, a motorcycle e-safety vest using haptic pods to give riders an extra sense of the traffic around them, which was recognised as top in the state and displayed at the Powerhouse Museum.

For three years, UTS Motorsports has been where that got tested. I joined the Formula SAE team in 2023, became Chassis Lead in 2024, and in 2025 took overall responsibility as Mechanical Lead for the design, build and integration of our electric competition car. That year we became the first UTS team in years to pass all technical scrutineering, completed every dynamic event, and recorded the team's first endurance finish since 2019. I drove the car myself. Along the way I led around twenty engineers, ran recruitment and procurement, and presented at SXSW Sydney in 2023 and again in 2025.

Since 2025 I have also worked as a data and race engineer with Tigani Motorsport in GT3 competition, which is where the mechanical side of me met the systems side. There I run trackside data and telemetry, configure Bosch and MoTeC systems, and have built the team's server, VPN, radio systems and the software that turns a session of in-car video and CAN data into per-driver stints and pit stop timings. University has filled in the rest: embedded hardware, PCB design, robotics, control software, thermofluids. Outside all of it I keep building for myself, from a postie bike with a phone-operated start to an engine rebuilt purely to test a gasket material.

What I want next is work where the design decisions and the physical outcome stay connected: full-system mechanical design on real hardware, with a team around me that I can help bring up.
